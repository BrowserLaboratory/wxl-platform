Informal fallacies are recurring patterns of reasoning that look like argument but fail on inspection. The catalogue is old and well-trodden: **ad hominem** attacks the arguer instead of the argument; **straw man** restates the opponent's position in a weaker form and refutes that; **false dilemma** presents two options when more exist; **slippery slope** chains unlicensed consequences from a modest premise; **hasty generalization** projects from a small or biased sample; **appeal to authority** substitutes a name for evidence. None of these are formal errors — the inferences could in principle hold — but in practice they substitute rhetorical pressure for support.

Wachsmuth and colleagues fold this tradition into their reasonableness sub-dimensions, treating fallacious moves as a quality deficit rather than a separate phenomenon. An argument that scores well on local relevance can still fail global acceptability if it leans on a straw man or a false dilemma.

## How ILL uses it

The `illogical_critic.md` persona keeps the standard list in working memory and surfaces each pattern as a Critical finding when it carries argumentative weight. A throwaway ad hominem in a casual aside is not the target; an ad hominem that stands in for refuting the opposing view is. ILL names the fallacy, quotes the move, and explains what evidence the prose would have needed instead.

**Known limits.** Labels can be applied too eagerly — much vivid prose uses hyperbole or selective example without intending an argument. ILL is calibrated to flag fallacies that load-bear, not every rhetorical flourish that resembles one.
